Screening Test

FMGE — the exam that decides if your degree works in India.

Every Indian who studies medicine abroad must clear the Foreign Medical Graduate Examination before registration. Pass rates sit around 10–15% for most countries. This is the single most important fact about studying abroad.

The rules

Who sits it, and when.

Eligibility+
Indian citizens with a primary medical qualification from a foreign institute, who had qualified NEET and meet the NMC eligibility rules for that course. The NMC-listing requirement applies to your university.
When can you appear+
Only after completing the full course and the 12-month internship abroad. You cannot shorten this by sitting early.
Format+
A computer-based test of 300 multiple-choice questions across pre-clinical, para-clinical and clinical subjects. Roughly half the questions are clinical. It is held twice a year.
NExT+
NExT is planned to replace FMGE and the NEET-PG style route with a single licensing exam. Until it is fully operational, FMGE remains the screening test — prepare for FMGE, and watch NMC notifications.
Preparation

What actually gets people through.

Start in year four, not after returning+
Students who pass usually begin structured revision 18 months before graduation, using Indian PG-entrance material. Waiting until you land back in India is the most common failure pattern.
Coaching budget+
₹60,000–1.5 L for a serious course. Treat it as part of the degree cost, not an optional extra. Free YouTube revision helps but rarely replaces a schedule and test series.
Subject weight+
Medicine, surgery, OBG and paediatrics carry the most clinical questions. Pre-clinical basics still appear. A pass needs breadth, not one strong subject.
If you fail+
Attempts are not capped, but every attempt costs six months and coaching money. Three failed attempts is the usual point where families should reassess the plan.
