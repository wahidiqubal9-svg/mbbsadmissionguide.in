Living

Hostel life — the costs and the first month.

Dormitory living abroad is survivable and cheap; it is also the fastest way to fail first year if you do not plan food, warmth and a study routine. Practical notes below.

Money

Real monthly numbers.

Hostel+
University dormitories run ₹3,000–6,000/month. Private shared flats are ₹8,000–15,000 in big cities. Dorms are usually 2–3 sharing with limited kitchen access.
Food+
Indian mess ₹6,000–10,000/month where it exists. Cooking yourself is cheaper but needs utensils and a shared kitchen — confirm kitchen access before choosing a dorm.
Other+
Insurance, phone, local transport, winter clothing and books add ₹4,000–8,000/month in year one. Winter kit is a one-time ₹15,000–25,000 in cold countries.
First month

Do these in week one.

Registration and documents+
Complete migration registration within the legal window, submit the university's document set, and store scanned copies in cloud storage plus one offline copy.
Food and warmth+
Locate the nearest Indian grocery and the mess on day one. Buy the winter coat before October, not after the first snow — prices and stock both collapse.
Study routine+
Fix a timetable in the first week covering the language year. Students who drift in year one rarely recover the vocabulary needed for clinical years.
Money transfer+
Use a bank or authorised dealer, keep every receipt, and avoid carrying large cash. Compare the exchange spread, not just the headline rate.
